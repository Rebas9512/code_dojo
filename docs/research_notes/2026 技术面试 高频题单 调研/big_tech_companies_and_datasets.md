# Big-Tech Company-Specific High-Frequency Coding Questions & Public Company-Frequency Datasets (as of 2026-11-22)

> Scope: US big-tech SWE coding interviews (Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Apple, Bloomberg) on leetcode.com (English), plus the public GitHub datasets that mirror LeetCode Premium company tags.
> Method note: The per-company "top problem" lists below were **computed directly** by downloading the raw CSVs from the two freshest GitHub mirrors (liquidslr snapshot committed 2026-10-21; snehasishroy snapshot "as of 12 July 2026", committed 2026-08-15) and sorting by their `Frequency` column. Problem IDs come from snehasishroy's `ID` column joined by URL slug. These are **crowd-sourced LeetCode company-tag data** (LeetCode Premium scraped by third parties), not official company statements. Interview-format facts are labelled **[OFFICIAL]** (company's own site), **[REPORTED-PRESS]** (news reporting), or **[CROWD/3P]** (prep sites, Glassdoor, LeetCode Discuss, Blind).

## Q1. Per-company coding interview loops, emphasis, and most-frequently reported LeetCode problems

### Takeaway
All six companies still run LeetCode-style DS&A coding rounds, but 2025–2026 brought two big shifts: AI-assisted coding rounds (Meta from Oct 2025; Google piloting a Gemini-assisted "code comprehension" round from H2 2026; Amazon's OA reportedly adding an AI-assisted repo question) and anti-cheating moves back to in-person rounds (Google, mid-2025). In the 2026 LeetCode company-tag data, the recent windows (30 days to 6 months) are dominated by the same generic "Top Interview" problems at every company (Two Sum, 3Sum, LRU Cache, Trapping Rain Water and so on). The company-specific signal shows up mainly in the all-time lists and in the problems that are distinctive to one company. Meta's classic list (1249, 408, 680, 314, 1650, 528...) is the clearest example.

### Inferences
- The Meta "2 mediums in ~35–40 min" heuristic is still consistent with Meta's official 2022 text (two problems, 35 coding minutes) and with 2026 third-party guides. The main 2025–26 change is that one onsite coding round may now be the 60-minute AI-assisted, multi-file round, and for E7+ it is the only coding round. A practice platform should add a "multi-part codebase / debugging" practice mode, not just algorithm puzzles.
- The shared head of every company's recent list (1, 2, 3, 4, 15, 42, 121, 146, 200, 560...) looks like a "LeetCode Top Interview" baseline. To tag problems by company in a way that matters, weight problems by **lift**: a problem's frequency at company X relative to its average across companies. The distinctive lists above are a quick approximation of that.
- The Google SQL and JS-easy entries and Meta's recent-window drift suggest that LeetCode's 2025–26 recent-window tags are noisier than the all-time list. They may be influenced by how LeetCode collects the "asked in interview" signal. A blend of all-time plus 6-month data, with a noise filter that drops Database, JavaScript and Pandas categories, is safer than any single window.
- Apple's data is too thin for frequency ranking (4 problems in 30 days, 26 in 3 months). For Apple, use the all-time list plus the design and bit-manipulation lean.
- Amazon's lists include very recent problem IDs (3371, 3413, 3914). This is consistent with OA questions being reproduced as new LeetCode problems, so Amazon OA practice should include recent-ID mediums that use greedy, sliding window and prefix sums.
- Google's harder reputation shows up only modestly in the data (17.3% Hard in 6 months, the same as Amazon's 17.5%). Its distinctive all-time problems are notably harder (410, 354, 85, 1944), and the 2026 pilot is moving toward code comprehension and debugging.

### Gaps
- Google's official careers interview-prep text could not be retrieved (the how-we-hire page fetched had no interview details). The Google round count, 45-min length and Google-Doc environment are confirmed only by third-party and press sources.
- No official Microsoft, Apple or Bloomberg interview-format pages were retrieved. Their loop details are crowd and third-party sourced.
- The 2026 Amazon OA "AI-assisted repo question" claim comes only from a LeetCode Discuss post seen via search snippet (403 on fetch). It is unverified.
- Meta AI-round scope (pilot vs. near-universal) remains conflicting across sources. Neither the Meta AI-round language list nor the model list is confirmed by Meta itself.
- Language rules for most companies are not officially specified beyond "use your best language" (Meta 2022) and "syntactically correct, no pseudocode" (Amazon). No official language list was found for the Amazon OA (its FAQ answer was not in the fetched content).
- No source explains *why* the 2025–26 recent-window LeetCode tags are dominated by generic problems. The contamination inference is based on the data patterns only.

## Q2. What LeetCode Premium company tagging provides (frequency buckets) vs. what is free

### Takeaway
Premium company tags let users list a company's tagged problems, sort them by frequency, and filter by recency window. Two independent 2026 scrapes use identical bucket names: **Thirty Days, Three Months, Six Months, More Than Six Months, All**. This is strong evidence that these are LeetCode's current windows. They replaced the older 6 months / 1 year / 2 years / all-time scheme. Frequency is shown as a relative 0–100 bar, not a raw count. The free tier does not get company grouping or frequency, although a company-count panel ("Trending Companies") is visible to the user.

### Cited Findings
- liquidslr's Oct-2026 snapshot stores each company as five files, `1. Thirty Days.csv`, `2. Three Months.csv`, `3. Six Months.csv`, `4. More Than Six Months.csv` and `5. All.csv`. Columns are `Difficulty, Title, Frequency, Acceptance Rate, Link, Topics` — [liquidslr Amazon folder](https://github.com/liquidslr/leetcode-company-wise-problems/tree/main/Amazon)
- snehasishroy's July-2026 snapshot independently uses `thirty-days.csv, three-months.csv, six-months.csv, more-than-six-months.csv, all.csv` with columns `ID, URL, Title, Difficulty, Acceptance %, Frequency %` — [snehasishroy amazon folder](https://github.com/snehasishroy/leetcode-companywise-interview-questions/tree/master/amazon)
- The snehasishroy README text still lists "Last 30 Days, Last 3 Months, Last 6 Months, Last 1 Year, All". No 1-year file exists, so the README is stale relative to the file layout — [snehasishroy README](https://github.com/snehasishroy/leetcode-companywise-interview-questions)
- The older scheme had files suffixed `_6months`, `_1year`, `_2year` and `_alltime` (200 companies; last commit 2023-04-01) — [krishnadey30 repo](https://github.com/krishnadey30/LeetCode-Questions-CompanyWise). A Blind user describes the recency dropdown as 6 months / 1 year / 2 years / all time (undated) — [Blind: leetcode frequency](https://www.teamblind.com/post/leetcode-frequency-gnnqwdww)
- Frequency is relative. In liquidslr, each file's top problem = 100.0 and the floor is 5.0 (Amazon all-time range 5.0–100). In snehasishroy, `Frequency %` takes coarse steps (e.g. Amazon 30-day: 100%, 75%, 62.5%, 50%). That pattern is consistent with normalizing small underlying counts. Neither dataset exposes raw report counts — computed from [liquidslr Amazon/5. All.csv](https://github.com/liquidslr/leetcode-company-wise-problems/blob/main/Amazon/5.%20All.csv) and [snehasishroy amazon/thirty-days.csv](https://github.com/snehasishroy/leetcode-companywise-interview-questions/blob/master/amazon/thirty-days.csv)
- [CROWD/3P] A 2026 review dated 2026-10-14 says LeetCode Premium costs $35/month or $159/year, and that Premium's company-specific questions can be sorted "by role, time or frequency". Its comparison table lists the free tier as having "no company grouping" — [PracHub LeetCode Premium review 2026](https://prachub.com/resources/leetcode-premium-review-2026)
- [CROWD] Blind users say LeetCode has never explained how tags are sourced, and that frequency counts look inconsistent across views — [Blind: how LeetCode comes up with company tagged questions](https://www.teamblind.com/post/how-leetcode-comes-up-with-company-tagged-questions-kes0wrux); [Blind: frequency ranking inconsistencies](https://www.teamblind.com/post/frequency-ranking-inconsistencies-on-leetcode-xneb0ktn)
- [CROWD] Some guides recommend filtering to the last 6 months or 1 year and then sorting by frequency — [Taro: best LeetCode Premium filters for company questions](https://jointaro.com/question/bC78DsNeHfidfH303yW7/best-leetcode-premium-filters-for-company-questions); [DEV: LeetCode premium company tags most people use them wrong](https://dev.to/alex_hunter_44f4c9ed6671e/leetcode-premium-company-tags-most-people-use-them-wrong-1blg)

### Inferences
- The "Trending Companies" counts the user saw (Amazon 2013, Google 2307, Meta 1382, Microsoft 1354, Bloomberg 1215, Apple 278) correspond to each company's **All** bucket size. They match the scraped `All` files within ~1–2% (e.g. Meta 1381 in Jul-2026, Bloomberg 1213 in Jul-2026). That implies the counts are free-visible metadata, while the per-problem list, frequency and window filters are paywalled.
- Because frequency is normalized per file and per window, values are not comparable across companies or windows. A platform should convert them to per-window ranks or percentiles before combining.
- The coarse step values in snehasishroy's recent windows suggest very small underlying sample sizes (likely single-digit report counts) for 30-day lists at most companies. Treat the 30-day bucket as near-noise.

### Gaps
- leetcode.com/subscribe returned 403, so LeetCode's own Premium feature list and free-vs-premium table could not be quoted. The exact free-tier visibility of company pages (whether the list is visible without frequency, or fully locked) is not confirmed by a primary source.
- No official LeetCode documentation was found on how company tags and frequency are collected (e.g. user "I encountered this in an interview" reports) or when the window scheme changed from 6mo/1y/2y to 30d/3mo/6mo/>6mo.

## Q4. Legal / ToS concerns with scraped LeetCode Premium company data

### Takeaway
LeetCode's Terms explicitly forbid crawling and scraping, assert ownership of all Content, forbid account sharing and forbid using the Service to "directly compete with LeetCode". So the act of producing these datasets violates ToS for the scraper's account. Re-using the derived tags (problem slug ↔ company ↔ frequency) in a private, personal tool is lower-risk than republishing them. None of the fresh datasets carries a license that grants reuse rights.

### Cited Findings
- LeetCode Terms (last modified 04/05/2024): "crawling," "scraping," or "spidering" any part of the Service are prohibited and "strictly forbidden". Also prohibited: "any process that operates while you are not logged into the platform" — [LeetCode Terms of Service](https://leetcode.com/terms/)
- The Terms say "all Content is our sole and exclusive property" and "All Our Content is copyrighted under United States copyright laws" — [LeetCode Terms of Service](https://leetcode.com/terms/)
- The Terms say users "must not use the Service in violation of any law or regulation, nor directly compete with LeetCode" and must not "allow any third party to use your account" — [LeetCode Terms of Service](https://leetcode.com/terms/)
- The Terms contain no provision that specifically addresses sharing Premium content (per a fetch of the Terms page) — [LeetCode Terms of Service](https://leetcode.com/terms/)
- The snehasishroy dataset is produced by logging into a Premium account and running a Selenium scraper, which the README documents — [snehasishroy README](https://github.com/snehasishroy/leetcode-companywise-interview-questions)
- liquidslr, snehasishroy and krishnadey30 have no LICENSE (GitHub API `license: null`). hxu296 is MIT — [liquidslr](https://github.com/liquidslr/leetcode-company-wise-problems); [snehasishroy](https://github.com/snehasishroy/leetcode-companywise-interview-questions); [krishnadey30](https://github.com/krishnadey30/LeetCode-Questions-CompanyWise); [hxu296 LICENSE](https://github.com/hxu296/leetcode-company-wise-problems-2022/blob/main/LICENSE)

### Inferences
- These are non-lawyer inferences, not legal advice:
  - The ToS binds the account holder who scrapes. A downstream user who only downloads a public GitHub CSV has not agreed to anything by doing so, unless they also use LeetCode. A personal-platform owner who does have a LeetCode account is a party to the Terms, so they should not run their own scraper against Premium pages.
  - A repo with no LICENSE grants no explicit reuse or redistribution rights by default, so bundling these CSVs into a public product or repo is riskier than private personal use. GitHub's guidance on unlicensed repos can be verified at https://choosealicense.com/no-permission/ (not fetched in this session).
  - The company↔problem mapping is largely factual metadata. Problem statements, editorial content and LeetCode's curated list compilation are more clearly protected. Lowest-risk design: store only slug, ID, title, company, window and normalized rank, link out to leetcode.com for problem text, and do not republish the full company lists publicly or build a competing public service.
  - The "directly compete with LeetCode" clause is the main exposure if the practice platform ever becomes public or commercial.

### Gaps
- No court case, DMCA notice or takedown history specific to these GitHub repos was found. Whether LeetCode has ever enforced against company-tag mirrors is unknown. The repos remain public with tens of thousands of stars as of 2026-11-22.
- The legal status of factual-compilation reuse (e.g. US *Feist* doctrine, EU database rights) was not researched with sources in this session.
